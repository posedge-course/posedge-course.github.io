Predict q

The clk lane is the clock: each column is one cycle, from one rising edge to the next. A flip-flop copies d to q at each rising clock edge and holds it in between. Cycles 0 and 1 are drawn for you. Set q for the rest.

That is how this course works: you predict what a piece of hardware will do, then a real simulation shows you. Posedge is a short, free course that goes from never having touched an FPGA to getting a design working on a board and reading its timing report. Every waveform here comes from simulating the code shown, and every report comes from AMD Vivado.

The levels

Thirteen levels, each a few short lessons, a project and a checkpoint; the last two take apart two real designs. Pass a level's checkpoint to open the next.
