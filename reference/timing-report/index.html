Reading a timing report

Every build in this course ends with a timing summary, timing_summary.rpt. The excerpts here come from two of them: msum16, which meets the board's 100 MHz clock with little to spare, and msum32, which misses it (lesson 8.2). In the Vivado window, the same report is under Open Implemented Design, Report Timing Summary.

The header

The design, the part it was built for, and the step: Routed means after place and route, with the delays of the real wires.

What cannot be checked

  • no_clock counts registers with no clock, and Vivado checks no path through them. It must be 0: if it is not, look for a missing or misspelt create_clock.
  • no_input_delay and no_output_delay count ports with no timing of their own, such as buttons and LEDs, and Vivado does not time paths from or to them. A button can change at any moment, so it goes through a synchroniser first.

The summary

  • WNS is the worst slack of any path. Positive, every path arrives in time; negative, it is how late the worst one arrives.
  • TNS adds up the negative slacks of the failing endpoints, and TNS Failing Endpoints counts them, out of TNS Total Endpoints.
  • WHS and THS are the same for hold. The last four columns are pulse width checks, on the clock pins of cells, which this course does not cover.
  • The line under the table says whether every constraint is met. msum16's are; msum32 has 5 failing endpoints out of 517, the worst 0.500 ns late.

The clocks

Each clock as the constraints describe it. The waveform {0.000 5.000} rises at 0 ns and falls at 5 ns; the period is 10 ns, 100 MHz. A wrong number here means a wrong create_clock, and every check below it is against the wrong clock.

One path's header

  • Slack is required time minus arrival time, worked out at the end of the path's lines.
  • Source is a flip-flop's clock pin and Destination a flip-flop's D, named after your code with _reg added.
  • Path Type says this is a setup check. The paths under Max Delay Paths are setup checks, and those under Min Delay Paths hold checks.
  • Requirement is the time allowed, one period. Data Path Delay is the time the data takes, split into logic, inside cells, and route, in the wires.
  • Logic Levels counts the cells between the two flip-flops: 14, seven of them CARRY4s.
  • Clock Path Skew is how much later the edge reaches the destination than the source. Here it is negative, which leaves the path a little less time. Clock Uncertainty is an allowance for jitter, the small random wobble in when each clock edge arrives.

The path, line by line

  • Location is where placement put each part: pin E3 for the clock, a clock buffer, and slices such as SLICE_X4Y74.
  • Delay type is a cell, with the pins its delay runs between, or a net, the wire to the next cell, with fo=, its fanout: the number of pins it drives.
  • Incr(ns) is each line's delay, and Path(ns) the total so far, from the clock's rising edge at 0 ns.
  • First comes the clock's own path, from pin E3 through the input buffer and the global clock buffer to the source flip-flop: 5.307 ns. Then the data: the flip-flop's clock-to-output time, 0.419 ns, then each net and cell.
  • The data reaches the destination's D at 14.912 ns: the arrival time.
  • Then the clock again, from the next rising edge at 10 ns, through the same buffers to the destination's clock pin: 15.015 ns. Clock pessimism removal adds back 0.259 ns: Vivado times the source's clock as slow and the destination's as fast, but the two share their start, which cannot be both. The uncertainty takes off 0.035 ns, and the flip-flop's setup line, here +0.109 ns, gives the required time, 15.347 ns.
  • The slack is 15.347 − 14.912 = 0.435 ns, the WNS of Listing 3.

When it fails

msum32's worst path is 0.500 ns late. Its data takes 10.559 ns, more than the 10 ns period and more than half of it in the wires, through 14 cells; the clock's skew and uncertainty and the flip-flop's setup, in the lines below the header, make the slack −0.500 ns. Vivado still writes a bitstream, but on the board the flip-flop at the end may take a wrong value. Give the path less to do between two edges, by pipelining it (lesson 8.4), or give it more time, with a slower clock if the design can have one.
