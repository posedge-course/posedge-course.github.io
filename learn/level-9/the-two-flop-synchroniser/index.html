9.2 The two-flop synchroniser

Teaches Synchroniser (circuit). About 6 minutes.

The cure for metastability is time. Put two flip-flops in a row on the clock that reads the signal. The first takes the asynchronous input and may hang; the second takes the first's output a whole cycle later, by which time the first has almost always settled. Everything else reads the second, which is all but certain to be a clean 0 or 1: it could hang only if the first had not settled in a whole cycle. The pair is a synchroniser, and Listing 1 is one.

Each clock and the logic it runs is a clock domain; a synchroniser belongs to the domain that reads. In Figure 1, the first flip-flop is lesson 9.1's model, and the second is level 2's dff.

What it costs, and what it cannot do

A change reaches sync_out two edges after it, or three if the first flip-flop settled to the old value and takes the new one an edge late. So two signals that change together on one clock may arrive an edge apart on the other. That is fine for a single level, such as a button, and wrong for a bus: an 8-bit count through eight synchronisers can read, for a cycle, as a mixture of old and new bits. And a pulse shorter than the reading clock's period can fall between two edges and never be seen. Lesson 9.3 deals with both.

(* ASYNC_REG = "TRUE" *) is an attribute, a note to Vivado on the two flip-flops: ASYNC_REG marks them as a synchroniser. Vivado then places them close together, leaving the first more of the cycle to settle, and its clock domain crossing (CDC) report (report_cdc), which lists the paths between clocks it treats as unrelated, recognises them. Level 4's debounce began with the same two flip-flops, for the button, but without the attribute: a design for the board should add it.

Question 2 of 3. Predict sync_out

This is a synchroniser whose first flip-flop is lesson 9.1's model of metastability and whose second is level 2's dff. async_in rises 0.5 ns before the edge that starts cycle 1, inside the model's window: the first flip-flop goes to X, and 4 ns later settles to its old value, 0.
