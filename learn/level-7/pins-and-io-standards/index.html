7.1 Pins and I/O standards

Teaches Pin constraint (primitive). About 5 minutes.

Lesson 0.3 showed that the code never names a pin: the constraints file does. Each port reaches the outside world through one package pin, one of the 324 balls under this chip, and the board's wiring joins some of those pins to its buttons, LEDs and connectors. Listing 1 is the start of the constraints file for this level's project, hello, which sends text to your PC over the board's USB cable: its clock and its two buttons.

A pin for each port, a standard for each pin

set_property PACKAGE_PIN D9 [get_ports btn] ties the port btn to pin D9, which the board wires to button BTN0. A bus takes one line per bit, with the bit in braces: [get_ports {led[0]}].

The file ends with set_property IOSTANDARD LVCMOS33 [get_ports {clk rst btn tx}], which gives each pin its I/O standard: the voltage its signals use and the way they are driven. On the Arty, the pins wired to these parts are powered at 3.3 V, so every pin in this course is LVCMOS33, 3.3 V CMOS. A port with no pin, or a pin with no standard, stops the build at the bitstream step: Vivado will not guess what your board has wired.

Digilent's master XDC

Digilent publishes a master XDC for the Arty: a line for every pin you are likely to use, each turned off with a #. You copy the lines you need, remove the #, and change each name to your port's. Its lines set the pin and the standard together, in a -dict list; the course's files write them as separate lines, which say the same.

Two things in it catch people out. Its LED names count from 0 where the board's labels do not: its led[0] is the LED marked LD4, as the note Sch=led[4] at the end of the line says. And a name that implies a direction is from the PC's point of view, not the FPGA's: the reference manual says so, and the _in or _out at the end gives the FPGA's side.

How many pins does hello use?

The report says 4.

Read the Bonded IOB row: 4, one for each port, clk, rst, btn and tx. The Fixed column says 4 too: the constraints file placed all four, so Vivado chose none of them. Available is 210, the pins of this package that can carry your signals.

Question 2 of 3. Fill the blank

Level 1's switch_adder puts low[0] on LED LD4. Digilent's master XDC lists the four single LEDs as led[0] on H5, led[1] on J5, led[2] on T9 and led[3] on T10, and each line ends with the board's own name for it: Sch=led[4] to Sch=led[7]. Fill in the pin.

vivado/projects/switch_adder/switch_adder.xdc, lines 12 to 13

12## low[0] to low[3] are LEDs LD4 to LD7.
13set_property PACKAGE_PIN (blank) [get_ports {low[0]}]
Show answer and explanation

The blank is H5.

The master XDC counts these four LEDs from 0, but the board labels them LD4 to LD7, as its Sch= notes say: LD4 is led[0], on pin H5. Reading led[4] as LD4 would find no such line, and a pin taken from the wrong line lights the wrong LED, or none.

Question 3 of 3. Fill the blank

hello's output tx carries characters from the FPGA to the PC, through the board's USB-UART bridge. Digilent's master XDC has two lines for that bridge: uart_rxd_out on pin D10 and uart_txd_in on pin A9. Fill in the pin for tx.

vivado/projects/hello/hello.xdc, lines 10 to 10

10set_property PACKAGE_PIN (blank) [get_ports tx]
Show answer and explanation

The blank is D10.

D10. Digilent names these lines from the PC's side, as its reference manual says: the PC receives (rxd) on the line the FPGA drives out (out), which is what tx is. uart_txd_in carries what the PC transmits, into the FPGA. So the FPGA's transmit line goes on the pin whose name says rxd.
