6.2 Adders and multiplexers

Teaches Carry chain (primitive), Wide multiplexer (primitive). About 6 minutes.

Lesson 6.1 built acc8's adder from a LUT2 for each bit and two CARRY4s. Adders and multiplexers are in almost every design, and the chip has parts made for both.

Carry chains

When you add on paper, each column waits for the carry from the column to its right. A CARRY4 is four columns of a carry chain: logic beside the LUTs that passes each carry straight to the next bit, without going through a LUT or the wiring between cells. CARRY4s join end to end, so a wide adder is one long chain, and each bit also needs a LUT to combine its two inputs before the chain takes over.

A counter only ever adds 1, and its next value depends only on its own bits. That leaves its LUTs almost nothing to do: the carry chain does the counting.

Wide multiplexers

Lesson 1.3's 4-to-1 multiplexer filled one LUT6: four data inputs and two select bits. An 8-to-1 multiplexer has eleven inputs, too many for one LUT. For this, the chip has fixed multiplexers after the LUTs: a MUXF7 picks between the outputs of two LUT6s, and a MUXF8 picks between two MUXF7s. Listing 1 is an 8-to-1 multiplexer: d[sel] is a bit-select whose number is a signal, so it picks the bit of d that sel names. Listing 2 is what Vivado built from it.

Each LUT6 is a 4-to-1 multiplexer on four of the eight bits of d, and the MUXF7 picks one of their two outputs. The MUXF7 is already there beside the LUTs, so the wider multiplexer costs one more LUT6, not a second layer of LUTs. The IBUF row is the eleven input pins, d and sel, and the OBUF row is y.

Questions

Question 1 of 4. Predict sum[8]

add8 adds two 8-bit numbers, a and b, into a 9-bit sum. Its top bit, sum[8], is the carry out of bit 7: 1 when a + b is more than FF. a and b are drawn in hexadecimal, a new pair every 10 ns step. Steps 0 and 1 are drawn for you. Set sum[8] for the rest.

Question 2 of 4. Read the report

Vivado built level 2's blink for the Arty A7-100T. Its 27-bit count adds 1 at every rising edge while run is 1, and the LED shows its top bit. Below is the Primitives table of its Utilization report: one row for each kind of cell, with how many the design uses.

vivado/reports/blink/arty-a7-100t/utilization.rpt, lines 180 to 189. Written by Vivado.

180+----------+------+---------------------+181| Ref Name | Used | Functional Category |182+----------+------+---------------------+183| FDRE     |   27 |        Flop & Latch |184| CARRY4   |    7 |          CarryLogic |185| IBUF     |    3 |                  IO |186| OBUF     |    1 |                  IO |187| LUT1     |    1 |                 LUT |188| BUFG     |    1 |               Clock |189+----------+------+---------------------+

How many CARRY4 cells does blink use?

Show answer and explanation

The report says 7.

Read the CARRY4 row. A CARRY4 is four bits of carry chain, so 27 bits need seven of them, the last one using three bits: six would cover only 24, and one for each bit would be 27. The FDRE row is count itself, and the single LUT shows how little else adding 1 needs: the carry chain does the counting.

Question 3 of 4. Read the report

mux16 is a 16-to-1 multiplexer: y is the bit of the 16-bit d that sel picks. Below is the Primitives table of its Utilization report: one row for each kind of cell, with how many the design uses.

vivado/reports/mux16/arty-a7-100t/utilization.rpt, lines 180 to 188. Written by Vivado.

180+----------+------+---------------------+181| Ref Name | Used | Functional Category |182+----------+------+---------------------+183| IBUF     |   20 |                  IO |184| LUT6     |    4 |                 LUT |185| MUXF7    |    2 |               MuxFx |186| OBUF     |    1 |                  IO |187| MUXF8    |    1 |               MuxFx |188+----------+------+---------------------+

How many LUT6 cells does mux16 use?

Show answer and explanation

The report says 4.

Read the LUT6 row. Each LUT6 is a 4-to-1 multiplexer on four bits of d, so 16 bits need four. Two MUXF7s each pick between two LUT6s, and the MUXF8 picks between the MUXF7s, so y comes out of the MUXF8.

Question 4 of 4. Put the steps in order

Vivado built mux16, the 16-to-1 multiplexer, from LUT6s, MUXF7s and a MUXF8. Put in order the cells that one bit of d passes through on its way to y.

  • An IBUF brings the bit of d in from its pin.
  • An OBUF drives the pin of y.
  • A MUXF7 picks between two LUT6s.
  • A MUXF8 picks between two MUXF7s.
  • A LUT6 picks one of four bits of d.
Show answer and explanation

In order:

  1. An IBUF brings the bit of d in from its pin.
  2. A LUT6 picks one of four bits of d.
  3. A MUXF7 picks between two LUT6s.
  4. A MUXF8 picks between two MUXF7s.
  5. An OBUF drives the pin of y.

Each LUT6 chooses among four bits of d, a MUXF7 then chooses between two LUT6s, eight bits in all, and the MUXF8 between two MUXF7s, all sixteen. The MUXF8 must come after the MUXF7s, because it picks between their outputs. The pin buffers come first and last, as in lesson 0.3.
