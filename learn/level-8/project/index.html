Level 8

Level 8 project Make a slow design meet timing

Write msum32_pipe in my_msum32_pipe.sv until msum32_pipe_tb.sv prints TEST PASSED, then compare what pipelining did to msum32's slack, its latency and its registers. About 50 minutes in simulation.

msum32 takes a 16-bit sample at every rising edge and gives the sum of the last 32. All 31 additions happen between two edges, and that one long path misses the board's 100 MHz clock (lesson 8.2). This project splits it into a pipeline (lesson 8.4) and checks the result twice: the testbench for the sums, and Vivado's reports for the timing.

The design

The ports of msum32_pipe
PortDirection and widthWhat it is
clkinput, 1 bitthe clock
dininput, 16 bitsa new sample at every rising edge
youtput, 21 bitsthe sum of 32 samples, four cycles later than msum32's

The sum of 32 samples of up to 65,535 needs 21 bits, so every register of the tree is 21 bits wide, as y is. The file you write is my_msum32_pipe.sv; the module inside is msum32_pipe, the name the testbench uses.

What you see when it works

  • The run prints TEST PASSED.
  • In the waveform, y_nonzero becomes 1 at the fifth rising edge after the one that takes the 1 into x[0]: one edge for each of s1, s2, s3, s4 and y. msum32 would have it in y at the first.
  • The timing: msum32 misses the clock at 5 of its 517 endpoints, the worst by half a nanosecond; msum32_pipe meets it with almost 7 ns to spare.
  • The price: 1,065 FDREs against 533, as every level keeps its sums. The LUT cells hardly change, 522 against 521, but msum32's small LUTs share LUT6s two at a time (lesson 6.4), so on the chip it fills 285 LUTs to the pipeline's 522: each report's Slice LUTs line.

If it fails

  • error: localparam must have a value., then syntax error: the command lacks -g2012. Add it.
  • Unknown module type: msum32_pipe: the module in my_msum32_pipe.sv has another name. It must be called msum32_pipe.
  • TEST FAILED: y=2080000, expected 2097120 at 380 ns: y comes a cycle early. A level of registers is missing, or a level is written with =, which merges it into the next (lesson 2.2).
  • TEST FAILED: y=2031585, expected 2097120 at 380 ns: y comes a cycle late: there is one register too many between x and y.
  • TEST FAILED: y=1048544, expected 2097120 at 380 ns: a level loses its carries. Every register of the tree must be 21 bits, wide enough for its sum.

These messages are from Icarus Verilog 12.

On the board (optional)

You need an Arty A7 and Vivado for this part. Everything above works without them.

The board steps use Vivado's project mode, as in the earlier projects. They build both designs for their reports: nothing shows on the board. Look for the same Design Timing Summaries as Listings 1 and 2 in your own timing summaries; your WNS may differ a little, because place and route can put cells elsewhere, but msum32 should still miss the clock and your pipeline meet it.

Your checklist

Simulation path

  1. Make a new folder and save the four files listed above into it. msum32.sv is the design that misses timing; msum32_pipe_tb.sv is the specification of the one you write: the same sums, four cycles later.
  2. Make a file my_msum32_pipe.sv with a module named msum32_pipe and the three ports in the table, the same as msum32's.
  3. Keep the last 32 samples as msum32 does: an array x of 32 registers of 21 bits, where at each rising edge x[0] takes din with five 0 bits on top and each other x[i] takes x[i-1].
  4. In the same always_ff block, add four levels of 21-bit registers: 16 in s1, each the sum of two neighbouring samples, then 8 in s2, each the sum of two neighbouring s1, then 4 in s3 and 2 in s4 the same way. Last, y takes s4[0] + s4[1].
  5. Run iverilog -g2012 -o pipe_sim msum32_pipe_tb.sv my_msum32_pipe.sv, then vvp pipe_sim, until it prints TEST PASSED.
  6. Open msum32_pipe_tb.vcd and add clk, din and y_nonzero. Near the end, after a long run of zeros, din is 1 for one cycle. Count the rising edges from the one that takes it into x[0] to the one that makes y_nonzero 1.
  7. Read "What Vivado built" above: each design's timing summary, then its cells. For each, write down WNS, the failing endpoints, and its FDREs.

On the board (optional)

  1. In Vivado, create an RTL project for xc7a100tcsg324-1 with msum32.sv and the saved msum32.xdc, and a second one with my_msum32_pipe.sv and msum32_pipe.xdc. These builds are for their reports: din and y go to headers with nothing plugged in, so you need not program the board.
  2. Run Generate Bitstream in each, then open each timing summary. Compare your WNS with the course's above: place and route can move cells, so expect it close, not exact.
