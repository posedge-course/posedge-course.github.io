8.4 Pipelining a failing path

Teaches Pipeline (circuit). About 6 minutes.

A path that is too long for the clock can be given less to do between two edges. Put a register in the middle of it, and the one long path becomes two short ones, each with a whole period. A design split this way is a pipeline, and each part of it between two registers is a stage.

msum32 adds 32 samples between two edges and misses 100 MHz (lesson 8.2). Listing 1, msum32_pipe, works out the same sum as a tree: pairs of samples into s1, pairs of those sums into s2, and so on, with registers after each level.

Each path now starts at one level's registers and ends at the next: one addition between two edges, where msum32's worst path went through several. That is what lets it meet the clock.

What it costs

The sum comes later. A sample reaches y only after it has passed every level, one edge each: two edges after it is on din for msum32, six for msum32_pipe. The cycles from a value going in to its result coming out are a design's latency, and the pipeline adds four. The registers cost too: every level of the tree keeps its sums.

Anything that travels beside the data must be delayed to match, as mul16_pipe's v_in was in lesson 6.3. A flag that marks which y is the sum of a new burst of samples, say, needs four more registers of its own.

Pipelining works where the logic splits into steps, like the additions of a tree, and it changes when results come out: whatever reads them must expect the new latency.

Read WNS: 6.976. Each path now does one addition between two edges, so even the worst leaves almost 7 ns of the 10 to spare, where msum32, with every addition in one path, was 0.5 ns short.

Question 3 of 3. Find the bug

msum32_pipe_bug should be lesson 8.4's msum32_pipe: each level of the tree adds pairs from the level before. Its testbench, the one msum32_pipe passes, fails: y is 1572840 where the sum of 32 samples of 65,535 is 2097120. Pick the line that is wrong.

Show answer and explanation

The bug is on line 20, for (int i = 0; i < 4; i++) s3[i] <= s2[2*i] + s1[2*i+1];. It should read for (int i = 0; i < 4; i++) s3[i] <= s2[2*i] + s2[2*i+1];.

The s3 line adds s2[2*i] to s1[2*i+1]: its second operand comes from the first level, a sum of two samples, where it should be s2[2*i+1], a sum of four. So each s3 holds six samples, not eight, and y 24 of the 32: 24 × 65,535 is 1572840. The levels before and after are right; only this line mixes two levels.
