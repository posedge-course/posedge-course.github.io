5.4 A state machine driving a datapath

Teaches Controller and datapath (circuit). About 6 minutes.

A design that computes something usually splits in two. The datapath is the registers and arithmetic that do the work. The controller is a state machine that tells the datapath what to do, and when. Listing 1 multiplies a by b the slow way: it adds a to product once a cycle, b times.

The controller

The controller has three states. In IDLE it waits. A pulse on start begins a job, and the controller goes to BUSY. It stays there until left, the additions still to do, is 0, then goes to DONE, where it stays until the next start. The line IDLE, DONE: gives two states one case line, so a start in DONE begins a job too. A start that comes while it is busy is ignored. busy and done come straight from the state.

The datapath

The datapath reads the state to know what to do. When a job starts, it clears product and keeps a and b in addend and left, so they may change afterwards. At each edge in BUSY while left is not 0, it adds addend to product and takes 1 from left. In every other state it holds its values, so product stays readable in DONE.

Figure 1 is 5 times 3. Three additions take four cycles in BUSY: the last one is spent finding that left is 0.

Start, busy and done

A pulse to start, busy while it works, done when the result is ready: many blocks are driven this way, and a controller that runs other blocks waits on their done. The decoders of levels 11 and 12 are built from controllers and datapaths like this.

Show answer and explanation

The bug is on line 38, if (start) begin. It should read if (start & (state != BUSY)) begin.

The controller ignores a start while busy, but the datapath's first if loads a new job on every start. So a start in the middle of a job clears product and reloads left while the controller carries on, and the two halves no longer agree about when the job began. Written if (start & (state != BUSY)) begin, the datapath follows the controller.
