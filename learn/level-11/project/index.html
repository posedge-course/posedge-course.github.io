Level 11

Level 11 project The decoder's four-bank reader

Write bank4 in my_bank4.sv until bank4_tb.sv prints TEST PASSED, then see what Vivado makes of four banks. About 45 minutes in simulation.

The decoder's layers two and three read four taps a cycle from maps stored once, in four banks (lesson 11.2), through two registers (lesson 11.3). Here you build that reader for a 9 × 9 map: 84 places in four banks of 21 rows, 81 of them used. The decoder's words are 99 or 189 bits wide; yours are W bits, 8 by default, which is enough to hold each position's number.

The design

The ports of bank4
PortDirection and widthWhat it is
clkinput, 1 bitthe clock
weinput, 1 bit1: write wdata at the rising edge
waddrinput, 7 bitsthe position to write, 0 to 83
wdatainput, W bitsthe word to write
baseinput, 7 bitsthe first of four positions to read, 0 to 80
lane0output, W bitstwo rising edges after base: the word at position base
lane1output, W bitsthe word at base + 1, at the same time
lane2output, W bitsthe word at base + 2
lane3output, W bitsthe word at base + 3

The file you write is my_bank4.sv; the module inside is bank4, the name the testbench uses. A new base can go in at every rising edge, and its four words come out two edges later, while the next bases are on their way.

What Vivado built

Listing 1 is the Slice Logic table of the course's build. The four banks, 672 bits in all, became 24 LUTs used as memory, distributed RAM (lesson 6.3), and no block RAM. Your banks are read at a clock edge, so block RAM was possible, but each holds only 168 bits, and Vivado built them from LUTs. The 68 flip-flops are the two registers each word goes through, 64 bits, and the two 2-bit registers that delay r, 4 bits. The timing summary says that every constraint is met.

What you see when it works

  • The run prints TEST PASSED: for every base from 0 to 80, in order and jumping about, the four lanes hold base to base + 3, two cycles after the base went in.
  • In the waveform, a base and its four words are always two edges apart, and the lanes always count up, whichever bank each word came from.

If it fails

  • syntax error, followed by Invalid module instantiation: the command lacks -g2012. Add it.
  • Unknown module type: bank4: the module in my_bank4.sv has another name. It must be called bank4.
  • TEST FAILED: base 14: lanes 16 17 14 15, expected 14 15 16 17 at 870 ns: the words reach the lanes in bank order. Lane j must take bank (r + j) mod 4, with the r of the base the words belong to: r through the same two registers as the words.
  • TEST FAILED: base 14: lanes 0 1 2 3, expected 14 15 16 17 at 870 ns: these are the next base's words, so the words come one register early. Each bank's word goes through its read register, then its output register.
  • TEST FAILED: base 14: lanes 14 15 12 13, expected 14 15 16 17 at 870 ns: banks 0 and 1 read row 3, where 16 and 17 are in row 4. A bank below r reads the next row.
  • TEST FAILED: base 14: lanes 18 15 16 17, expected 14 15 16 17 at 870 ns: bank 2 read row 4. Only the banks below r read the next row, not bank r itself.
  • TEST FAILED: base 1: lanes 3 4 1 2, expected 1 2 3 4 at 890 ns: the lanes turn the wrong way. Lane j takes bank (r + j) mod 4, not (j − r) mod 4; the two agree only when r is 0 or 2.
  • TEST FAILED: base 14: lanes 14 15 x x, expected 14 15 16 17 at 870 ns: the bank number did not wrap round at 4. A plain 1 is 32 bits wide, so r + 1 can be 4, and there is no bank 4. Add a two-bit constant, such as 2'd1.

These messages are from Icarus Verilog 12.

On the board (optional)

You need an Arty A7 and Vivado for this part. Everything above works without them.

This project has nothing to see on the board: the build is for its reports, as in level 8's project. The steps use Vivado's project mode, as in the earlier projects.

Your checklist

Simulation path

  1. Make a new folder and save the two files listed above into it. bank4_tb.sv is the specification: it writes every position with its own number, then reads base 14, every base from 0 to 80, and every base again in a jumping order.
  2. Make a file my_bank4.sv with a module named bank4, a parameter W that defaults to 8, and the nine ports in the table.
  3. Give it four memories of 21 words of W bits, one per bank. At a rising edge where we is 1, position waddr goes into bank waddr mod 4, at row waddr / 4: the low two bits of waddr pick the bank, the other five the row.
  4. Split base the same way, into its row q and its bank r. Bank b reads row q + 1 if b is less than r, and row q otherwise (lesson 11.2).
  5. Read each bank through two registers: its read register, which takes the word at the row at each rising edge, then an output register (lesson 11.3). Send r through two registers as well, so that it arrives with the words it belongs to.
  6. Lane j takes the word of bank (r + j) mod 4, using the r that went through the two registers. Add two-bit constants such as 2'd1: a sum of two-bit numbers wraps around at 4 by itself, but a plain 1 is 32 bits wide, and a sum with it does not wrap.
  7. Run iverilog -g2012 -o bank4_sim bank4_tb.sv my_bank4.sv, then vvp bank4_sim, until it prints TEST PASSED.
  8. Open bank4_tb.vcd and add clk, base and the four lanes. Find where base is 14 (0E), and count two rising edges to where the lanes hold 0E, 0F, 10 and 11, as in lesson 11.2's Figure 1.
  9. Read "What Vivado built" above, and find what holds the four banks' words.

On the board (optional)

  1. In Vivado, create an RTL project for xc7a100tcsg324-1 with my_bank4.sv and the saved bank4.xdc, make bank4 the top module, and run implementation. This build is for its reports: the lanes go to the Pmod headers and the rest to the chipKIT header, with nothing plugged in.
  2. Open the utilisation report and compare it with the course's above, then check in the timing summary that every constraint is met.
