11.3 Data two cycles late

Teaches Read latency (primitive). About 6 minutes.

A block RAM reads at the rising edge: the word for an address comes a cycle later (lesson 6.3). It also has an optional output register, one more register inside the block. With it the word comes a cycle later still, but it leaves the block early in its cycle, which gives the logic after it more time. The decoder's memories are read through two registers like these, so a word arrives two rising edges after its address: their read latency is two cycles.

Everything travels together

A word on its own is not enough. Layer two also needs to know, for each word:

  • which group of taps it belongs to;
  • whether its tap fell outside the grid: the hidden maps are stored with no border of zeros, so such a tap is masked, its product forced to 0;
  • whether it is the empty fourth lane of a group of 3;
  • which position it is for, and, for the lanes, the bank order to undo.

All of that is known when the address goes out, two cycles before the word arrives. So each of those signals goes through two registers too, a delay line as in lesson 4.3, and reaches the arithmetic together with its word. A signal that is one register short arrives a cycle early, beside the word of the tap before it, so each word meets the next tap's signal.

From there the work goes on in steps, one register apart. If the address went out in cycle n, the products come in cycle n + 3, their sums in n + 4 and n + 5, and the running total in n + 6. After the last group of taps, the bias is added once, ReLU applied and the result written, in n + 7. A six-stage delay line carries the flags alongside, so that each stage sees its own data's.

One tap a cycle

tap_pipe (Listing 1) is conv_row's convolution worked out one tap a cycle, with the row in a memory read through two registers. mask says a tap is inside the row, t which tap it is, and run that a tap went out. Each goes through two registers and meets its word as mask2, t2 and run2 (Figure 1).

Questions

Question 1 of 3. Predict d2

Lesson 11.3's tap_pipe has the row 4A (hexadecimal) in its memory: positions 1, 3 and 6 hold 1, the rest 0. addr is the address of the tap issued in each cycle, in hexadecimal; F is 1111, −1, and its read takes position 7, addr[2:0]. Cycles 0 and 1 are drawn for you: the words of addresses before the window. Set d2, the word at the end of the two read registers, for the rest.

Question 2 of 3. Predict done

Lesson 11.3's tap_pipe near the end of a row. run is 1 while taps are being issued, and t is the number of the tap issued in each cycle. done is 1 for one cycle when a position's decision comes out. Set done for every cycle.

Question 3 of 3. Find the bug

tap_pipe_bug should decide as lesson 11.3's tap_pipe does: conv_row's decisions, one tap a cycle. Its testbench fails at the first position of the row 25 (hexadecimal): it decides 1 where the logit is −2. Pick the line that is wrong.

Show answer and explanation

The bug is on line 78, assign wt = (t1 == 2'd0) ? 10'(w0) : (t1 == 2'd1) ? 10'(w1) : 10'(w2);. It should read assign wt = (t2 == 2'd0) ? 10'(w0) : (t2 == 2'd1) ? 10'(w1) : 10'(w2);.

The weight is chosen by t1, the number one register short of the word: it belongs to the tap issued a cycle later. So each word gets the next tap's weight: position 0's x[0], a 1, gets 3, the weight of x[1], instead of −1, and the logit comes out as 2. The tap's number must meet its word, as t2.
