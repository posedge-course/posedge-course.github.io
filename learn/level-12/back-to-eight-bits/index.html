12.2 Back to eight bits

Teaches Requantiser (circuit). About 6 minutes.

A hidden layer's sums are 32-bit numbers, but the next layer reads 8-bit activations. So after each hidden layer a requantiser turns every finished sum back into 8 bits, in four steps:

  1. ReLU: a negative sum becomes 0. Only the low 25 bits go on; with the decoder's weights, no sum needs more.
  2. Multiply by M (m in the code), the channel's multiplier, a whole number.
  3. Shift right by S bits, which divides by 2 to the power S. Adding half of 2 to the power S first makes this rounding to the nearest whole number, not down.
  4. Clip: a result over 255, the largest 8-bit value, becomes 255 instead of wrapping round to a small number. This is saturation.

Together, steps 2 and 3 multiply by the fraction M / 2 to the power S, which scales a sum down to the range the next layer reads, with nothing but a whole-number multiply and a shift.

With M = 3 and S = 2, the sum 13 gives (39 + 2) / 4, which is 10.25, so 10. A negative sum gives 0, and 400 would give 300, which saturates to 255. Each channel has its own M, and each layer its own S: the decoder's are 15, 20 and 19.

One a layer

Each engine but the last has one requantiser; in the decoder's first board build, each was one DSP block. It takes only finished sums, one output channel a cycle while the engine works on its next group of output channels, and it is done long before their sums are ready, so it never holds the engine up.

In small

requant (Listing 1) does the four steps in two stages: the product at one rising edge, the result at the next. Figure 1 has M = 3 and S = 2, three quarters of each sum. The sum 340 gives exactly 255; 341 gives 255.75, which rounds to 256 and is clipped, and sat says so.

Question 3 of 3. Find the bug

requant_bug should clip a result over 255 to 255, as lesson 12.2's requant does. Its testbench fails on the sum 256 with m 4: sat is 1, as it should be, but q is 0 instead of 255. Pick the line that is wrong.

Show answer and explanation

The bug is on line 32, q <= r[7:0];. It should read q <= (r > 41'd255) ? 8'd255 : r[7:0];.

r[7:0] keeps only the low 8 bits of the result. 256 is 1 0000 0000, so its low 8 bits are 0, and 400 would give 144: a value too big for 8 bits wraps round. q must take 255 whenever r is over 255, the same test that sat makes.
