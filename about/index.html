About Posedge

Posedge is a short, free, interactive course on FPGAs. It takes you from never having touched one to a small design working on a real board, and to reading the timing report that says why it works.

Who it is for

Students and junior engineers who can already program. The new part is thinking in hardware: everything happens at once, and a design that simulates correctly can still do nothing on the board. Good free tutorials on the language exist already, so this course spends its time on what happens after the code is written: what synthesis builds from it, constraints, timing and signals that cross between clocks.

How it works

Thirteen levels, in order. Levels 0 to 10 go from what an FPGA is to interfaces; levels 11 and 12 take apart two neural decoders for quantum error correction that run on the course's board. Each level has four lessons of about five minutes, a small project and a checkpoint. What you learn comes back later in short reviews, spaced further apart each time you get one right: small problems to solve, such as a waveform to predict or a bug to find, never flashcards.

How it is checked

  • Every listing of SystemVerilog on the site is a file with a testbench. Verilator checks it, Icarus Verilog runs it, and pages show that file, never a copy typed in by hand.
  • Every waveform is drawn from a simulation of that code.
  • Every report excerpt comes from a build in AMD Vivado 2025.2 for the Digilent Arty A7-100T.
  • Board facts come from Digilent's reference manual and master XDC files, and Vivado's behaviour from AMD's user guides. Any claim no tool can check is listed for the author to review.

The site

A static website: no accounts, no tracking, no cookies and no scripts from other sites. Your progress stays in your browser (what is stored). The fonts are Archivo and Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono, under the SIL Open Font License, served from this site. Digilent's master XDC files are used under the MIT License.
